Address Inconsistencies & Directory Documentation

INTEL_SUMMARY

The Deadquarters archive documents a pattern of address inconsistencies at L.A. Care Health Plan. Public directories continued to list an abandoned construction site as the official headquarters, which coincided with difficulties in legal service and inconsistent information for Medi-Cal members and state regulators.

FORENSIC ARCHIVE
THE L.A. CARE ADDRESS INCONSISTENCY

L.A. Care's public directories continued to list an abandoned construction site (1055 W. 7th St.) as its headquarters, while its executives operated from 1200 W. 7th St. This multi-year inconsistency left the public-facing address inconsistent with L.A. Care's actual location. The condition of the 1055 office was documented and photographed in the field by professional process servers from ABC Legal during attempts to serve court papers.

SUMMARY: L.A. Care used its correct 1200 W. 7th St. address for enrollment materials, while continuing to list the "Ghost Office" address in directories used by service-dependent populations and for legal correspondence. This directory inconsistency raises questions under HSC 1367.27 and may implicate Knox-Keene requirements for accurate provider directories.

Frequently Asked Questions

01What is a "Ghost Office"? A physical address listed as a functional business domicile that is actually abandoned, non-existent, or structurally incapable of housing the business operations claimed.
02Why does L.A. Care maintain a ghost address? One possibility is that it functions as a jurisdictional barrier to service of process and separates executive leadership from the public-facing directory infrastructure. This site does not have visibility into L.A. Care's internal decision-making.
03Is this a violation of law? Health and Safety Code section 1367.27 requires health plans to maintain accurate provider directories. Continuing to list an abandoned construction site as a primary corporate headquarters appears inconsistent with that requirement, though only a regulator or court can make a final determination.
