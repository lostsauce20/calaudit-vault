Definition of Form DHCS 4521

Form DHCS 4521 is the official Medi-Cal Claim Form for Beneficiary Reimbursement used by individuals to request a financial refund for out-of-pocket medical, pharmacy, or dental expenses paid during a retroactive or pending eligibility window.

SUMMARY

The DHCS 4521 Guide provides instructions for navigating the official Conlan reimbursement mechanism. This page serves as a repository for the required filing forms (DHCS 4521 and STD 204) and outlines the submission strategy for documenting administrative default.

Reimbursement Protocol

The DHCS 4521 (Conlan Claim) is the legal mechanism for requesting a refund when you paid out-of-pocket for services Medi-Cal should have covered. Claims are processed by the DHCS Beneficiary Service Center (BSC).

Submission Strategy

Prevent administrative loss and establish a verifiable timeline.

1. Original Wet Ink Signature Requirement

Use blue or black ink only. Submit the original form with your original signature — photocopies, scans, or digital signatures will be rejected by DHCS.

2. USPS Certified Mail Proof of Delivery

USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt gives you a tracking number and a physical signature confirming delivery. This is your primary weapon if your health plan or the state misses their respective adjudication deadlines.

3. Compiling the Complete Conlan Claim Record

Never send your only copy. Make a full photocopy or clear digital scan of every page of your signed packet, every receipt, and the envelope before you seal it.

FILING DEADLINE

Conlan Claim Submission Timeline

  • Standard timeline: The beneficiary claim packet must be received within 1 year from the date of service or 90 calendar days from the date when the beneficiary's Medi-Cal card (BIC) was issued, whichever is later.
  • Retroactive eligibility: If you are approved retroactively, you have up to 90 days from the Notice of Action (NOA) showing Medi-Cal enrollment or 1 year from the service date (whichever is later).
  • Carrier disinformation: Managed care plans frequently attempt to enforce a strict "90-day" window for member-submitted claims, misapplying the standard 90-day provider billing rule to deny Conlan claims. Knowing and citing the state-level 1-year rule is your primary defense.
  • Late submissions: Late submissions beyond the 1-year/90-day mark may occasionally be processed if you can document "good cause" (e.g., severe illness or official administrative error by DHCS).

⚠️ Important: Missing the 1-year/90-day statutory window can result in an irreversible claim denial. Submit immediately upon receiving eligibility and secure your USPS Certified Mail receipt as proof of submission date.

PROCESSING DEADLINE

AB 3275: 30-Day Mandate

  • Deadline: If submitting a reimbursement request directly to your Managed Care Plan (L.A. Care), the plan must pay or deny within 30 calendar days of receipt under HSC § 1371 / AB 3275. For claims submitted directly to DHCS, the court-mandated processing timeline is 120 calendar days (Welfare & Institutions Code § 14019.3).
  • If the health plan fails to respond in 30 days: The plan is in statutory default. You can immediately file a DMHC complaint or escalate to Small Claims.
  • Double Timeline Protection: While DHCS's internal Conlan unit has 120 days to process clean claims, your managed care plan cannot use this to delay their own 30-calendar-day default under AB 3275.

✅ Provider Recoupment Backstop: If a provider refuses to refund your out-of-pocket payment for a covered service, DHCS is authorized under Welfare & Institutions Code § 14019.3(g) to pay you directly and recoup (deduct) the full amount from the provider's future Medi-Cal payments, forcing provider compliance.

Provider refuses refund? File a complaint with DMHC: 1-888-466-2219 or dmhc.ca.gov (opens in a new window)

Exhibit C: The Protective Government Claim

To neutralize L.A. Care's "jurisdictional shield" defense, you should file a formal, protective Government Claim under the Government Claims Act (GCA) after the 30-day default. While Gov Code § 905(e) expressly exempts claims for public assistance or services rendered on behalf of public-assistance recipients, local plans will aggressively litigate this classification in small claims. The safest litigation-risk mitigation is dual-track preservation: present a timely, protective GCA claim to the proper § 915 recipient (the Clerk of the Board), explicitly reserving your § 905(e) position, while concurrently pursuing the administrative grievance track.

Legal Frame: A defaulted Conlan claim is a breach of a statutory duty (AB 3275). By presenting a protective Government Claim (citing Gov Code § 910) and waiting for the 45-day rejection/deemed-rejection window, you establish Administrative Exhaustion. This transforms the default into an actionable, trial-ready debt for Small Claims Court, bypassing potential procedural dismissals.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions: DHCS 4521 Form

What is the DHCS 4521 form?

The DHCS 4521 form is the official Medi-Cal claim form used by beneficiaries to request out-of-pocket reimbursement for medical expenses under the Conlan v. Bonta court decision.

How do I get a refund if I paid out-of-pocket for ADHD medications like Vyvanse or Adderall?

If you paid out-of-pocket for prescription stimulants (such as Vyvanse, Adderall, or generic alternatives) because your pharmacy claimed it was not covered, you are entitled to a full refund under the Conlan process. However, because all pharmacy benefits were carved out to Medi-Cal Rx as of January 1, 2022, your claim packet must be routed directly to the Medi-Cal Rx Conlan Unit rather than your standard HMO plan.

To file a pharmacy Conlan claim, submit a completed Form DHCS 4521 and Form STD 204 (Payee Data Record) along with your original pharmacy cash receipt (which displays the drug name, dosage, and price paid) within 1 year of the payment date. If a Prior Authorization (PA) was required, ask your physician to request a retroactive PA from Medi-Cal Rx to satisfy the clinical medical necessity requirement.

Where do I mail the DHCS 4521 form?

Mail the completed DHCS 4521 form and all required evidence via Certified Mail with Return Receipt to: DHCS-Beneficiary Service Center, P.O. Box 138008, Sacramento, CA 95813-8008.

How long does Medi-Cal Conlan reimbursement take?

Under HSC § 1371 and AB 3275, managed care plans must pay, contest, or deny a reimbursement claim within 30 calendar days of receipt. However, for Conlan claims submitted directly to the DHCS Beneficiary Services Unit, the court-mandated processing timeline is 120 calendar days under Welfare & Institutions Code § 14019.3. If your health plan fails to act on its own 30-day window, they are in default.

How long do I have to file a Medi-Cal Conlan reimbursement claim?

California regulations allow up to 1 year from the date of service or 90 calendar days from the date when the beneficiary's Medi-Cal card (BIC) was issued, whichever is later, to submit your complete Conlan claim packet.

What is the MC 220 form?

The MC 220 form is the California Department of Health Care Services (DHCS) Authorization for Release of Information, which allows beneficiaries to authorize disclosure of medical, mental health, and educational records to third-party advocates or legal representatives.

What is the MC 382 form?

The MC 382 form is the Appointment of Authorized Representative form, which is used to formally designate an individual or organization to handle your Medi-Cal eligibility, enrollment, and administrative appeals.

What is a DHCS reimbursement default?

A default occurs when your managed care plan fails to pay, contest, or deny a reimbursement claim within the 30-calendar-day statutory deadline mandated by AB 3275, or when DHCS fails to process a Conlan claim within its court-mandated 120-day window under WIC § 14019.3.

How do I prove a reimbursement default in court?

Provide the USPS Certified Mail Return Receipt (proving delivery) and a sworn log stating that no determination was received within 30 days. This shifts the burden of proof to DHCS.

Can I download the DHCS 4521 PDF here?

Yes. You can download a blank DHCS 4521 PDF form directly from the vault (opens in a new window) for immediate filing, alongside supporting forms MC 220 (opens in a new window) and MC 382 (opens in a new window). Why do I need the STD 204 Payee Data Record for Medi-Cal reimbursement?

The State of California requires the STD 204 form (Payee Data Record) in lieu of IRS Form W-9 to process any payment to an individual. DHCS cannot issue your Conlan reimbursement check without a completed and signed STD 204 on file.

If your managed care plan refuses to process your reimbursement or blows statutory deadlines, this may constitute a Knox-Keene violation under California health law. To review non-compliance, access our full statutory rights field manual.

CRITICAL WARNING

The Conlan "Kick-Back" Trap

DEATH BY CHECKBOX

State adjudicators leverage microscopic technical omissions to return initial out-of-pocket submissions, attempting to reset their legal clock. Check every validation box before shipping your evidence packet.

  • The "Idiot Boxes": Check every verification field. Leaving Section 4 blank (California Residency Confirmation) triggers immediate automated rejections.
  • The "Proof of Payment" Trap: Standard itemized invoices are not enough. You must append clear evidence of asset liquidation (zero-balance store register receipts, cleared bank ledger lines, or canceled checks).
  • Signatures and Ink: Wet signatures must match your primary files exactly. Use dark ink to eliminate automated scanning/legibility flag errors.
  • The Pro-Tip: Mail all packets exclusively via USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt. Under AB 3275, the state has exactly 30 calendar days from delivery to process or contest your claim. The tracking code locks the clock.
The Wrong Card
HMO / Managed Care Card

Do NOT use regional cards like L.A. Care, Health Net, or Molina on DHCS 4521 submissions. Conlan adjustments are overseen by state-level agents, not the private managed care plans.

The Right Card
State BIC Card (White w/ Poppy)

You must submit an unblurred copy of your official state Benefits Identification Card (BIC). If missing, generate a fresh digital verification printout via BenefitsCal.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions: The Conlan Route

▶ What is a "Conlan Claim"?
Established under the Conlan v. Bonta precedent, this administrative pathway permits Medi-Cal recipients to secure direct reimbursement for out-of-pocket costs forced by managed care enrollment barriers or networks operating in bad faith.
▶ Can you sue L.A. Care directly in Small Claims Court?
Yes. While DHCS handles the programmatic Conlan administrative track, managed care plans retain independent civil liability when their specific operational negligence or pharmacy network blockades cause direct financial harm to members. Small Claims Court (limit: $12,500) is accessible without an attorney.
▶ What is the statutory response deadline under AB 3275?
AB 3275 mandates that a definitive decision — approval, contest, or denial — must be issued within 30 calendar days of receiving a verified out-of-pocket Conlan reimbursement packet. The USPS Certified Mail delivery date locks the clock.
▶ What happens if DHCS misses the 30-day deadline?
As logged on this page, the state routinely defaults on its timelines. Documenting these breaches with certified mail tracking creates the formal evidentiary baseline required for state fair hearing challenges or subsequent court writs. A missed deadline is itself a statutory violation.
▶ What documents are required for a valid Conlan packet?
A valid packet requires: a completed DHCS 4521 form with all sections filled including Section 4 (California Residency Confirmation), a clear copy of your state BIC card (white with poppy), itemized receipts plus proof of payment (zero-balance register receipts, cleared bank lines, or canceled checks), and wet signatures in dark ink. See the DHCS 4521 Filing Guide for the full checklist.
▶ Does DHCS vs. L.A. Care matter for which route to pursue?
They are parallel, independent tracks. The Conlan DHCS 4521 route targets the state for reimbursement of out-of-pocket costs. The Small Claims route targets L.A. Care directly for their operational negligence and network failures. Both can be pursued simultaneously — and documented failures on one track strengthen the other.
