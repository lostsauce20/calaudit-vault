Call the Car 5-Mile Pharmacy Ride Cap — APL 22-008 Violation

Recorded in July 2026. This exhibit documents the Call the Car pharmacy-ride scheduling interface enforcing a fixed “Within 5 miles” boundary while the user repeatedly attempts to change the distance selector. DHCS guidance expressly prohibits MCPs from imposing mileage restrictions on transportation to medically necessary services.

What this evidence shows

The recording captures the pharmacy-search portion of the Call the Car transportation application. The user repeatedly taps the distance control and scrolls the interface, but the range remains fixed at “Within 5 miles.” The pharmacy results shown on screen remain confined to that five-mile boundary.

Finding: This is a flat mileage restriction in the transportation scheduling interface. DHCS APL 22-008 and its accompanying FAQ do not permit an MCP to impose a blanket mileage cap on transportation to medically necessary services. The five-mile hard limit documented here is therefore a violation of the transportation requirements cited below.

The same account had already received two approved pharmacy trips exceeding five miles on March 16, 2026. That prior history further shows that the later five-mile boundary was a newly restrictive condition rather than an inherent limit of the transportation benefit.

Why this violates APL 22-008

APL 22-008, page 3: DHCS requires managed care plans to ensure there are no limits on receiving authorized, medically necessary NEMT. The same page separately requires medically appropriate NEMT for pharmacy prescriptions prescribed by Medi-Cal providers or authorized under Medi-Cal Rx.

APL 22-008, pages 7–8: DHCS also requires NMT necessary to obtain medically necessary Medi-Cal services, expressly including pharmacy services and round-trip transportation for prescription pickup when the medication cannot be mailed directly to the member.

DHCS FAQ, Question 3: “MCPs shall not impose any mileage restrictions for transportation to medically necessary services.” A plan may separately apply DHCS-approved utilization controls or qualifying network rules, but those are not permission to substitute a fixed five-mile boundary for an individualized transportation determination.

The video does not merely show a pharmacy list sorted by distance. It records repeated attempts to change the transportation distance control while the system continues to enforce “Within 5 miles.” On the recorded interface, the mileage restriction itself is the operative limitation.

Methodology and scope

  • The recording captures one continuous eight-second interaction with the pharmacy-distance control.
  • The visible interface continues to show “Within 5 miles” throughout the recorded sequence despite repeated attempts to change the setting.
  • The finding concerns the user-facing flat mileage restriction documented by the recording; access to source code or backend configuration is not required to observe that restriction.
  • DHCS permits certain utilization-management and network controls, but its FAQ separately prohibits blanket mileage restrictions. Those are distinct rules.
  • The prior seven-mile pharmacy ride history is corroborating context and can be evaluated against the linked approval record.
