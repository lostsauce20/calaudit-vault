How to Sue Your Health Insurance Plan in California Small Claims Court

Quick Summary: 5 Steps to Sue a California Health Plan ($12,500 Max)

  1. Exhaust Administrative Remedies: Submit a 30-day formal grievance or DMHC complaint.
  2. Calculate Total Damages: Include unpaid Conlan claims, out-of-pocket medical visits, and travel expenses up to the $12,500 individual limit.
  3. Draft Form SC-100 (Plaintiff's Claim): Cite breach of contract and statutory violations under Health & Safety Code § 1367 and AB 3275.
  4. File Fee Waiver (Form FW-001): Get court filing fees waived 100% if receiving Medi-Cal or low-income assistance.
  5. Serve the Agent for Service of Process: Formally serve the health plan’s corporate entity via certified process server or Sheriff.

Navigating California health plans (such as L.A. Care Health Plan, Kaiser Permanente, or Anthem Blue Cross) can be exhausting when valid reimbursement claims are wrongfully delayed or denied. When internal grievances stall out, California Small Claims Court provides a fast, binding, and cost-effective legal forum to hold health maintenance organizations (HMOs) accountable.

This guide focuses on monetary claims that fit within California Small Claims Court's applicable limit. Claims involving significant personal injury, medical malpractice, wrongful death, larger damages, or other complex relief may involve additional pre-suit requirements or different civil procedures. Consider obtaining legal advice before choosing a forum.

// 1. Small Claims Limits & Jurisdiction

Under California Code of Civil Procedure §§ 116.110–116.950, natural persons (individuals) can sue managed care plans for up to $12,500 in monetary damages. Corporate entities filing as plaintiffs are limited to $6,250.

Filer Type Maximum Claim Cap Attorney Representation Allowed?
Individual Plaintiff $12,500 No (Consultations only)
Business / Corporate Plaintiff $6,250 No (Employee representative allowed)

Important Rule: Neither party is allowed to bring an attorney to the actual Small Claims trial hearing. This eliminates the legal advantage massive health insurance corporations usually hold in Superior Court.

⚠️ WARNING: Are You Suing a Public Entity Health Plan?

Do not file Form SC-100 yet if your plan is a Public Entity!

Health plans like L.A. Care Health Plan (Local Initiative Health Authority for Los Angeles County), CalOptima, and IEHP are public government agencies—not standard private corporations. Under the California Government Claims Act (Gov. Code § 900 et seq.):

  • You MUST file a formal administrative Government Claim directly with the health plan first. Under Gov. Code § 911.2, the deadline is 1 year from the date of the financial harm/denial for reimbursement and contract-type claims like this one (the shorter 6-month deadline only applies to personal injury or property damage claims).
  • You must wait for their official Notice of Rejection (or allow the 45-day statutory review window to expire) before you are legally allowed to file in Small Claims Court.
  • Failing to file this pre-lawsuit claim will result in your Small Claims case being instantly dismissed.

Note: If you already filed a Government Claim and received a rejection letter, attach that rejection letter directly as "Exhibit A" to your SC-100 filing to prove exhaustion of public entity remedies!

// 2. Statutory Citations to Copy-Paste on Form SC-100 (Item 3)

When completing Item 3 of your Form SC-100 (Plaintiff's Claim), explicitly reference California statutory provisions to establish breach of duty and statutory default:

  • Health & Safety Code § 1367: Requires health care service plans to provide basic health care services, establish accessible network standards (such as those analyzed in our documented ghost-network evidence), and operate in good faith.
  • Health & Safety Code § 1371.4 / AB 3275: Mandates immediate reimbursement for emergency services and care, prohibiting unauthorized delays or bad-faith claim denials for emergency treatment.
  • Conlan Reimbursement Rights: Entitles Medi-Cal beneficiaries to 100% cash reimbursement (pursuant to the Form DHCS 4521 rules) for out-of-pocket payments incurred during pending eligibility or network provider processing delays.

Example Language for Form SC-100:

Defendant breached contractual and statutory duties under California Health & Safety Code § 1367 and AB 3275 by failing to reimburse Plaintiff for covered out-of-pocket medical care, emergency services, and associated travel expenses incurred. Plaintiff has exhausted administrative remedies. Total damages: $3,115.73.

// 3. Finding the Correct Corporate Legal Entity for Service

A common mistake is serving a local medical clinic instead of the registered health plan entity. You must serve the official Agent for Service of Process listed on the California Secretary of State (BizFile Online) portal.

Example Entity Mapping:

  • L.A. Care Health Plan: Registered legally as Local Initiative Health Authority for Los Angeles County (1200 W. 7th Street, Los Angeles, CA; review our analysis of documented address discrepancies).
  • Kaiser Foundation Health Plan: Served via designated corporate legal officers listed on the Secretary of State registry.

// 4. How to Submit Form FW-001 for 100% Fee Waivers

Small claims filing fees range from $30 to $75 depending on claim size. However, if you receive Medi-Cal, SSI, CalFresh, or qualify as low-income, you can submit Form FW-001 (Request to Waive Court Fees) along with your SC-100.

  • Upon review, the court clerk issues Form FW-003 (Order on Court Fee Waiver), waiving 100% of filing and process server fees.

// Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How long do I have to file a lawsuit against my health plan?

It depends on whether your health plan is a public entity or a private one.

If your health plan is a public entity — such as a Local Initiative Health Authority or county-operated managed care plan — you must first file a Government Tort Claim under Government Code § 910 et seq. before you can sue. This initial claim must be filed within one (1) year of the financial harm/denial for reimbursement and contract-type claims under Government Code § 911.2 (the shorter six-month deadline applies only to personal injury or property damage claims). Once the claim is formally rejected with written notice under § 913, you have six (6) months from the date of the rejection letter to file in court under Government Code § 945.6(a)(1). If the public entity fails to act and does not provide written notice, the claim is "deemed rejected" 45 days after delivery, and you have two (2) years from the accrual of the cause of action to file in court under Government Code § 945.6(a)(2). However, filing within the shorter six-month window after rejection is still highly recommended to avoid notice or delivery disputes.

If your health plan is a private entity, the statute of limitations for breach of a written contract under California Code of Civil Procedure § 337 is four (4) years from the date of the breach. However, exhausting internal grievances and DMHC remedies first is still strongly recommended before filing suit.

How do I serve the defendant and when does proof of service need to be filed?

Service method and timing are governed strictly by Code of Civil Procedure § 116.340. If the defendant is in the same county, they must be served at least 15 days before the hearing date. If they are in a different county, they must be served at least 20 days before the hearing date. For substituted service, you must complete service at least 25 days (in-county) or 30 days (out-of-county) before the hearing date because service is not deemed complete until 10 days after mailing.

No matter the service method, the signed Form SC-104 Proof of Service must be filed with the court clerk at least five (5) days before the hearing date. File it as soon as service is confirmed — do not wait until the day before.

What evidence should I bring to the court hearing?

Bring three organized physical copies (one for you, one for the commissioner, and one for the defendant's representative) containing:

  1. Copies of filed Form DHCS 4521 / Conlan claims.
  2. Itemized medical bills displaying diagnostic/CPT codes.
  3. Proof of out-of-pocket payments (bank statements, receipts).
  4. Administrative grievance logs, denial letters, and rejected government claims.
  5. USPS certified mail delivery confirmation for your Government Tort Claim.
