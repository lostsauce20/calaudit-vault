TACTICAL MANUAL

Filing an OCR Privacy Complaint

The HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR Part 160 and Subparts A and E of Part 164) protects all "individually identifiable health information." When a covered entity transmits your Protected Health Information (PHI) to unauthorized third parties — even family members — they have committed a reportable breach.

This manual provides the forensic roadmap for filing a complaint with the Office for Civil Rights (OCR) using a real-world violation at Inland Psychiatric Medical Group as the primary dataset.

// CASE STUDY: INLAND PSYCHIATRIC MEDICAL GROUP BREACH

Case Study Metrics: Inland Psychiatric Medical Group Breach

SUBJECT: Inland Psychiatric Medical Group (IPMG)
INCIDENT DATE: May 25–26, 2026
VIOLATION TYPE: Unauthorized Disclosure of PHI via SMS

On Monday, May 25, 2026, I contacted Inland Psychiatric Medical Group to schedule a telehealth intake for complex medication management. I explicitly provided the intake coordinator with my current, active cell phone number. The coordinator looked up my profile using my health plan Member ID, which evidently pulled up an outdated, historical profile containing an old contact number belonging to my father.

The coordinator never verified that an incorrect phone number was on file. The clinic transmitted my protected health information (PHI) - specifically my confidential psychiatric intake forms and telehealth appointment access links - via SMS text message to my father's phone number without my authorization or consent, starting on Monday, May 25 at 12:52 PM and again on Tuesday, May 26 at 9:08 AM.

My privacy was compromised, but to ensure I could receive care, I completed the intake paperwork after it was forwarded to me. On Tuesday, May 26, 2026, at 11:49 AM - exactly 41 minutes prior to my 12:30 PM appointment - I verbally contacted the clinic's front desk to explicitly confirm they had everything and to request the video link, which I had not received on my device. Despite this live confirmation call, the clinic failed to send the video link to my correct number, resulting in a severe delay and immediate disruption to the start of my medical care.

Frequently Asked Questions

[01] What is a HIPAA violation?
[A]: A HIPAA violation occurs when a covered entity (healthcare provider, health plan, or clearinghouse) fails to comply with any aspect of the HIPAA Privacy, Security, or Breach Notification Rules, such as unauthorized disclosure of Protected Health Information (PHI).
[02] Is there a time limit to file an OCR complaint?
[A]: Yes. A HIPAA complaint must be filed within 180 days of when you knew (or should have known) that the act or omission occurred. OCR may extend this limit if you can show 'good cause'.
[03] Does it cost money to file a HIPAA complaint?
[A]: No. Filing a complaint with the Office for Civil Rights (OCR) is completely free.
