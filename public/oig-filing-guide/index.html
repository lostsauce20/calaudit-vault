Reporting Corporate Pharmacy Fraud & Billing Manipulation

This guide documents the procedural steps for reporting corporate fraud, billing manipulation, and severe beneficiary harm involving government funds (Medi-Cal Rx) to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office of Inspector General (OIG).

// CASE STUDY: CVS PHARMACY #9785 BILLING MANIPULATION

SUBJECT: CVS Pharmacy Store #9785 (Garfield Beach CVS, LLC)
INCIDENT DATE: April 20–21, 2026
VIOLATION TYPE: Manual Reversal of Authorized Medi-Cal Rx Claims

This case documents the systematic use of authorized billing access to deceptively reverse government-funded medical transactions. Electronic logs confirm that Medi-Cal Rx authorized and paid for the claim three times; however, pharmacy staff manually reversed these payments to manufacture a "cannot fill" state, causing a 16-hour treatment lapse and severe physical harm.

Frequently Asked Questions

What qualifies as "Pharmacy Fraud"?

In this audit context, it includes manual reversal of authorized government payments, falsifying electronic records to refuse dispensing, and system abuse to manufacture a "cannot fill" state to bypass Medi-Cal Rx approvals.

Is OIG reporting different from a Board of Pharmacy complaint?

Yes. OIG investigates the misuse of government funds and beneficiary harm involving federal programs (Medi-Cal/Medicaid). The Board of Pharmacy focuses on professional licensing and clinical safety violations.

Can I remain anonymous when reporting to the OIG?

Yes. The OIG provides options for confidential or anonymous reporting, though providing your identity can significantly assist investigators in verifying and substantiating your claims.

What evidence should I gather before filing an OIG complaint?

Gather transaction logs showing authorized payments, electronic records of reversals, timestamps of the incident, names of responsible parties, and any prior reports made to internal or other regulatory channels such as DHCS or the Board of Pharmacy.

What happens after I submit a complaint to the OIG?

The OIG reviews the submission and issues a confirmation. Depending on the severity and documentation quality, it may open a formal investigation, refer the matter to another agency, or log it for pattern analysis across multiple complaints.
