EDITORIAL STUDY

Understanding the Selected Timeframes

Published timeframes still require case-specific review

DMHC materials describe 30 days for standard grievances, three days for qualifying urgent grievances, and appointment-access standards that vary by request type. Those timeframes do not answer whether a particular plan, request, grievance, exception, or extension fits the selected category. Treat the calculation as a record-review aid, not a legal conclusion.

Business days, calendar days, and disclosed assumptions

Appointment-access standards use hours or business days, while the grievance selections use calendar days. This tool performs arithmetic in Pacific Time and treats Monday through Friday as business days without modeling holidays. Because official measurement instructions, definitions, receipt times, plan records, clinical exceptions, amendments, or closures may affect the result, check current sources before treating the estimate as operative.

What happens after the selected timeline date passes

Preserve the original request, proof of receipt, plan correspondence, and any clinical records. Depending on the facts, DMHC guidance may allow a consumer complaint or an Independent Medical Review. Urgent matters may qualify for immediate DMHC assistance. The editable draft identifies the selected timeframe without deciding eligibility for a remedy; verify the process directly with the regulator.

SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

What This Calculator Can and Cannot Establish

The calculator can reproduce arithmetic for one selected timeframe and create an editable draft. It cannot determine which regulator governs a plan, whether a request is urgent, whether an exception or extension applies, when a plan legally received a submission, or whether a response satisfies the governing standard.

DMHC states that standard grievances generally must be resolved within 30 days and that urgent issues may be eligible for immediate assistance. Current official instructions and the plan record control over this calculator's output.

CalAudit's California Health Plan Timeline Calculator is a public, client-side, and free tool designed to make the selected timeline visible. It generates a reproducible calculation and an editable draft that users can compare with their own records, the governing rule, and current agency guidance.

FORENSIC FAQ

DMHC & Knox-Keene Timeline Questions

▶ How long does a California health plan have to respond to a grievance?
For the DMHC grievance track implemented by this calculator, the standard selection uses 30 calendar days and the expedited or urgent selection uses 72 hours. Whether that track applies to a particular matter depends on the plan, urgency, receipt, exceptions, extensions, and case-specific facts, so verify the result against current DMHC guidance and the plan record.
▶ What is the DMHC 30-day grievance timeline?
The calculator's standard grievance option applies a 30-calendar-day timeframe from the Pacific Time timestamp you enter. The output is a date estimate under the disclosed assumptions, not a determination that the plan legally received the grievance at that exact time or that no exception or extension applies.
▶ How long does an urgent grievance take under Knox-Keene?
The expedited grievance option in this tool uses a 72-hour timeframe under the selected HSC § 1368.01 track. The calculator does not decide whether a grievance qualifies as urgent; verify urgency criteria and current agency guidance before relying on the estimate.
▶ What are California timely-access standards for health-plan appointments?
This calculator includes selected DMHC appointment-access categories measured in either hours or business days, including urgent care and non-urgent primary-care, specialist, mental-health or substance-use-disorder, and ancillary-service selections. The result depends on the category chosen and the calculator does not determine whether an exception, extension, or different standard applies.
▶ How long can I wait for a specialist appointment in California?
The non-urgent specialist option in this calculator uses a 15-business-day timeframe under the selected DMHC timely-access track. Business-day calculations count Monday through Friday and do not model holidays or exceptional closures, so compare the estimate with current DMHC instructions and your plan's records.
▶ Does this calculator apply to PPO, ERISA, or fee-for-service Medi-Cal plans?
Not automatically. The tool is built around selected DMHC-managed-care timelines. If you choose CDI PPO, ERISA self-funded, or fee-for-service Medi-Cal, the calculator warns that a different regulatory track may apply and does not produce the DMHC timeline output without further verification.
▶ Does this tool require personal medical or identifying information?
No. The calculation and draft-generation code run in the browser, and this tool's JavaScript does not send the entered plan name, treatment description, or dates to a CalAudit endpoint. Avoid entering identifiers or clinical details you do not need for the calculation.
