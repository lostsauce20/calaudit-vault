VERBATIM COMPLAINT (SCROLLABLE)
I am reporting three related instances of fraudulent and retaliatory conduct by Uber Technologies, Inc., resulting in an unrecovered loss of $77.88.

1. FRAUDULENT TRIP CHARGE — On February 9, 2026 at 10:23 PM, I was charged $110.79 for an Uber Courier trip (driver: JOEL) with identical pickup and drop-off addresses (9801 Airport Blvd, Los Angeles, CA 90045). The trip data shows 50 miles traveled in 10 minutes, which is physically impossible. This trip was never requested by me. The charge was split across Uber Cash ($77.88) and my Visa card ($32.91). When I reported this to Uber as a serious incident, Uber responded that "the time frame to review this trip has passed" and refused to investigate further. Uber never restored the $77.88 in Uber Cash consumed by this fraudulent trip, and has remained completely silent on that portion of the loss — likely because Uber Cash cannot be recovered through a bank chargeback, leaving consumers with no recourse outside of Uber's own (unresponsive) support.

2. RETALIATORY ACCOUNT RESTRICTION AND FRAUDULENT CLAWBACK ATTEMPT — After I successfully disputed the $32.91 card charge via chargeback, Uber restricted my account. Uber then attempted to recover the $32.91 by misrepresenting it as a $9.99 Uber One subscription charge — an amount that does not match the chargeback, and which Uber framed using a promotional rate ($5.99) I had previously been offered but never accepted. This repackaging of a disputed charge as an unrelated subscription fee is fraudulent misrepresentation. Uber offered no communication or explanation throughout this process. The account restriction was lifted only after both my credit card issuer (Wells Fargo) and PayPal ruled in my favor — confirmed by the PayPal dispute resolution date — demonstrating the restriction was used as leverage to coerce me into abandoning legitimate fraud claims.

3. UNAUTHORIZED USE OF STALE PAYMENT AUTHORIZATION — Uber exploited a dormant PayPal billing agreement established in 2022 that had seen no activity for years. When I added a funded LendingClub (Radius Bank) card to my PayPal account for an unrelated purpose, Uber immediately charged that card a $9.99 "Uber One" subscription fee without my authorization. I had no active Uber One subscription and had not agreed to any new subscription terms. Uber's use of a years-old dormant authorization to charge a newly funded payment method constitutes unauthorized billing under the FTC's Negative Option Rule.

PATTERN OF CONDUCT — Throughout this entire dispute, Uber never responded substantively to any of my complaints. They refused to investigate the fraudulent trip, never acknowledged the account restriction, never explained lifting it, and never restored the $77.88 in Uber Cash. This pattern of deliberate silence appears designed to exhaust consumers and avoid accountability for charges that cannot be disputed through traditional banking channels.

I have screenshots of the trip receipts, Uber's dismissal email, LendingClub bank statement, PayPal dispute record with resolution date, the original 2022 PayPal billing agreement, and Uber trip data export as supporting documentation.

Frequently Asked Questions

01
What is "Uber Cash"?
It is store credit that exists outside the protections of the Fair Credit Billing Act. Banks cannot typically initiate chargebacks for Uber Cash balances, which is why Uber prioritizes its use for fraudulent charges.
02
Can Uber restrict my account for a chargeback?
Yes. Uber frequently uses account restrictions as a retaliatory tactic to coerce consumers into abandoning legitimate fraud claims. Documentation of this behavior is critical for your FTC report.
03
How does "Impossible Trip Data" occur?
It is often a result of courier-side exploits where trip progress is falsified or manipulated. Uber's refusal to investigate these clear data anomalies is a core component of our audit.
